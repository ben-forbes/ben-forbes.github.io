Benjamin Forbes

PhD Student, Mechanical Engineering · University of California, Los Angeles

Hey, I’m Ben. I build robotic systems designed around the human. Some of that means giving robots human capabilities, like a sense of touch or a clinician’s skill with a laryngoscope. The rest is building policies that let robots work more closely with people, like shared autonomy for a two-arm robot you control remotely through a passthrough headset.

Before UCLA, I earned my B.S./M.S. at Northwestern in four years while swimming in the Big Ten, and raced at the 2021 Olympic Trials.

Research

Tactile sensing

H2Opti: Underwater Tactile Sensing

Biomechatronics Lab, UCLA · Provisional patent, 2025

A fabric-covered touch sensor that lasted 400 abrasion cycles (standard ones: 100 and 226), plus a diffusion policy that replans the moment it feels contact.

My part: Sensor design and the haptic search policy.

Shared autonomy

Human-Centered Bimanual Teleoperation

Human Fusions Institute (UCLA, Case Western, Cleveland State) · IEEE Haptics Symposium poster, 2026

Two robot arms you control remotely through a passthrough headset, with touch feedback and shared autonomy that adapts to each operator.

My part: Controls team lead. Wrote the teleoperation controller and run the shared autonomy study.

Medical robotics

Autonomous Robotic Laryngoscopy

UCLA Head & Neck Surgery and EndoSight Systems · Manuscript in preparation

A robot that steers a flexible scope to the vocal cords on its own, using on-board vision and a diffusion policy learned from experts. 1st place at Sandler and Knapp, 2026.

My part: Designed, built and tested the robot, and wrote the control policies.

Before UCLA

Capstan-Amplified Electroadhesive Clutches

Center for Robotics and Biotechnology, Northwestern · npj Robotics, 2026

Wrapping an electroadhesive clutch around a capstan multiplies its holding force, for robots that are strong but still backdrivable.

My part: The capstan model and test rigs.

Robotic English Wheeling

Advanced Manufacturing Processes Lab, Northwestern · Journal of Manufacturing Systems, 2024

A desktop robotic English wheel that automates artisan sheet-metal forming.

My part: Force sensing, robot toolpaths, and a motorized anvil wheel.
